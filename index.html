Understanding Board Feet & Spray Foam Physics

A board foot is the standard industry unit of measure for spray foam insulation volume. One board foot represents a volume of 1 square foot covered at a thickness of 1 inch (12" × 12" × 1" = 144 cubic inches).

Closed-Cell vs. Open-Cell: For outdoor structures, metal buildings, vans, and basements, closed-cell foam (2.0 lb/cu ft) is the industry standard because its closed cellular structure is impervious to liquid water and acts as an official Class II vapor retarder at 2 inches thickness (perm rating < 1.0). Open-cell foam (0.5 lb/cu ft) expands aggressively at lower cost, but absorbs water and must never be applied directly against exterior metal siding where condensation forms.

Temperature Sensitivity: Spray foam chemical tanks MUST be warm (75°F to 85°F) for proper chemical expansion. Spraying with cold tanks causes poor yield, gummy unreacted chemicals, and off-ratio dispensing.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How many board feet of spray foam do I need for a 12x24 garage?
A 12×24 garage with 9-foot walls has roughly 650 sq ft of wall area and 290 sq ft of ceiling area (total ~940 sq ft). Sprayed at 2 inches of closed-cell foam, this requires 1,880 board feet (plus 10% waste = ~2,060 board feet), which equals four Froth-Pak 600 kits.
Does spray foam insulation prevent condensation in metal pole barns?
Yes. Condensation in metal buildings occurs when warm humid interior air contacts cold uninsulated metal exterior panels. Applying at least 1.5 to 2.0 inches of closed-cell spray foam directly onto the metal panels isolates the metal, seals all air infiltration gaps, and completely eliminates the condensation point.
